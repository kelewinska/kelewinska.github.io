I am a remote sensing scientist with over 15 years of experience leveraging satellite data to investigate, monitor, and understand environmental dynamics.

My primary scientific interests and expertise lie in (but are not limited to) analyzing long time series of remotely-sensed optical data. Over the past few years, my research has focused on grassland ecosystem dynamics, particularly changes in ground cover fractions (green vegetation, non-photosynthetic vegetation, and bare soil) and their relationships with environmental and meteorological conditions. More recently, I have expanded my interests towards agricultural ecosystems, exploring long-term changes in extensively managed landscapes. In addition, I am also strongly interested in examining how data quality and observation density influence the robustness and reliability of results.

To date, I have had the privilege of working alongside some of the exceptional Earth Observation scientists in Europe and the United States, contributing to projects funded by the European Commission, NASA, and the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG), and collaborating with environmental experts.

My geospatial experience is complemented by strong data processing and computational skills, including HPC environments. I am versed in a broad range of commercial and open-source tools, including ENVI/IDL, GEE, QGIS, and FORCE, as well as scripting and programming languages.

In those (rare) moments when I am outside my office habitat, I am most likely to be found cycling, or fixing bikes / working on my next bike project (n+1)… and at the end of a day, there is nothing better than to sit down with a book and a mug of (green) tea… happiness is to be found in quiet moments and simple things.
